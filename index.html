About

A little about me.

UFC vet. Coach. Dad. Arkansan. The fight career is part of the story — but on the mat, what matters is what we work on today.

I'm TJ Brown. Born in Pine Bluff, raised in Arkansas, and I've called Little Rock home for over a decade. I'm one of a handful of Arkansans to ever make it to the UFC — and as far as I know, the only one of us currently teaching out of the state. That's not a flex; it's just rare. It means if you want to work with someone who's actually fought at the highest level, you don't have to leave home to find it.

I came up at Westside MMA in Little Rock under Coach Roli Delgado, who later promoted me to BJJ black belt. I had a twelve-year career as a pro fighter — competed in the UFC from 2020 to 2024 — but honestly the best part of the journey has been the people I've gotten to work with along the way. Teaching is where I'm at my happiest.

Day-to-day I'm running private sessions, holding pads and mitts, putting people through 30- and 60-minute workouts, and working with whoever walks in — six years old or sixty, total beginner or active competitor. No age matters. If you want to put in honest work, we'll find a way.

The Story

Why I coach.

There's a longer story underneath all this — and it's why teaching is more important to me than fighting ever was.

I was eighteen, off-track at the start of college football, and about to be a father for the first time. I walked into a gym in Little Rock not really knowing what I was doing. A couple months later I won my first amateur fight in ten seconds. I was hooked.

I grew up without my dad around. I didn't meet him for the first time until I was 32 years old. So when my son Kyler showed up, I made one promise — that he'd never wonder where I was. That promise has shaped everything else: how I train, how I coach, why I stayed sober, why I do this for a living.

"It's a beautiful thing to be able to break that chain. My son is never gonna ask those questions." — TJ, on UFC.com

Coaching is how I pay it forward. Every kid that walks into one of my classes, every adult that finally gets brave enough to try this stuff — that's where the work actually happens. The fight career was great. This part is better.

FAQ

Good questions.

Stuff people ask me all the time. If yours isn't here, just shoot me a message.

I've never trained before. Is that okay?
More than okay — it's my favorite kind of student. The vast majority of people I teach are total beginners. Wear something comfortable, bring water, and show up. I'll handle the rest.
Is there an age limit?
No age matters. I work with kids as young as six and adults well into their sixties. Younger kids are about discipline, listening, and having fun. Adults get whatever pace works — technique, pad work, conditioning, or just learning how to move better. Parents are welcome to watch every session.
How long are the sessions?
Either 30 minutes or a full hour, depending on what you're after. A 30-minute slot is great for a focused pad/mitt round or a quick conditioning hit. The 60-minute session is the full mix — pad work, technique, live rounds or grappling, and conditioning. You can also book MMA-style workouts that combine pads, weights, and agility.
Do you focus on pad and mitt work?
Yes — pad and mitt work is one of my specialties. It's how I learned, how I trained at the pro level, and it's one of the best tools out there for teaching technique, building combinations, and getting in real conditioning at the same time. If that's what you're after, you're in the right spot.
What should I wear?
Athletic clothes, no shoes on the mat. If we're doing BJJ, I can lend you a gi. Bring water and a towel. That's it.
Where are the sessions held?
Little Rock, Arkansas. I'll send you the exact address when we lock in your time. For seminars or out-of-state work, I travel — just ask.
How much does it cost?
Depends on the format (private, small group, fight camp, seminar) and how often you want to train. Send me a booking request with your goals and I'll come back with options that make sense for you.
Are you still fighting?
Off and on. Most of my time goes to coaching now. If I'm in camp I'll let you know — but the lessons keep rolling.
Do you run seminars at other gyms?
Yes — and it's one of my favorite things to do. I'll come to your gym for a pad/mitt clinic, a striking or BJJ seminar, fight prep work, a kids clinic, or a talk & Q&A. Half-day, full-day, or weekend formats. In-state, out-of-state, or pro-team consults. Reach out through the booking form and tell me what your group needs — I'll put a proposal together with travel and pricing.
