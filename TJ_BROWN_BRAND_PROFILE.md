## 2. The Story — In TJ's Own Voice

The narrative arc that runs through every piece of TJ's media — and what the website needs to honor — is **a small-town Arkansas single dad who turned a wrong turn into a UFC career and used the platform to break the cycle for his son.**

The major beats:

**The fork in the road.** TJ told the UFC: *"About eight years ago. I made some mistakes at the start of college football and wasn't headed in a good direction. I found out I had a son on the way and had to make something happen. I found Westside MMA, started training and in a couple months took my first fight and won by knockout in 10 seconds. I was hooked."* That 10-second KO over Adrian Walker (actually his 2nd pro fight) is the origin moment.

**Breaking the chain.** TJ grew up without his father in his life — only met him for the first time in June 2022, at age 32. The headline on his UFC.com feature literally reads *"TJ Brown Broke The Chain."* He has said: *"It's a beautiful thing to be able to break that chain. I just remember growing up, wishing I had a dad around… my son is never gonna ask these questions."* He raises Kyler on his own.

**Putting Arkansas on the map.** He and longtime training partner / friend Bryce Mitchell are the two UFC fighters who came up out of the Westside MMA pipeline in Little Rock under Roli Delgado. TJ frames himself as a flag-bearer for Arkansas MMA: *"We have a lot of talent coming out here from Arkansas, and hopefully with me and Bryce and Roli and all of us together, we can kind of lead the way and show that we have something to prove down here."*

**Sobriety / discipline.** Going into the Elkins fight in Oct 2023, TJ publicly went sober: *"I'm on my fourth, going on fifth, month sober. I haven't had a drink in so long and that's got a lot to do with me performing better and where my head is now…  I pride myself on being this great father and I could see how I wasn't. I was beginning not to spend the time with him that I was. I wanted some consistency and that was a change I needed to make."*

**Self-image.** He told UFC.com in 2020: *"I think I'm one of the good guys. I'm a family man, I'm very disciplined and a hard-working guy and I am somebody I believe somebody can look up to. I want to lead the way."* This is the brand position — clean, accountable, role-model fighter, not a trash-talker.

**Heroes** (per UFC.com Q&A): Jose Aldo, GSP. Favorite grappling technique: arm triangle. Favorite striking technique: head kick. Both reflect his actual record.

## 5. Training Background

- **Westside MMA**, 806 S. Izard St., Little Rock, AR 72202 — home gym. Where it started and where TJ still teaches/trains. Run by **Roli Delgado** (UFC/Bellator vet, his BJJ professor). Other instructors: Matt Hamilton, Borris Washington, Jeremiah Cullum.
- **Glory MMA** (Lee's Summit, MO) under James Krause — listed on his Wikipedia training history (2021–present per Wiki). TJ had to step away in early 2023 after Krause's suspension. He has been publicly diplomatic about it ("really weird vibe… nothing but good to say about James Krause").
- **Tulum, Mexico camp** — did the lead-up to the April 2023 Algeo fight there with a community of world-class training partners.
- **Cornermen / coaches** he's named publicly: Roli Delgado (BJJ, lead corner), Bryce Mitchell (corners him in big fights).

## 9. Voice, Tone & Brand Pillars

Pulled from his own recurring quotes — these are the language and themes the site should *sound like*:

**Pillars**

1. **Family man first.** *"I'm a father first. Single father. My son comes first."* Every commercial piece — coaching, marketing, sponsorship — should be framed as service to family/community, not chest-beating.
2. **Break the chain.** Cycle-breaking. He grew up without a dad; Kyler doesn't have to. Personal accountability is the through-line.
3. **Arkansas / Little Rock pride.** "Arkansas will definitely be in the house." He carries the state, not just himself.
4. **Quiet confidence, no trash talk.** *"There's levels to this game"* / *"I want to welcome him to the UFC"* — he asserts hierarchy without disrespecting opponents. He has gone out of his way to be diplomatic even about people who've gone sideways (e.g., James Krause).
5. **Earned discipline.** Sobriety, training in Mexico, decade of grinding regional shows. He didn't get handed anything.
6. **Role model.** *"I do believe I can fill that role… somebody who can be looked up to. I want to lead the way."*

**Tone of voice on the website**
- Warm, plainspoken, Southern, never preachy.
- Confident but humble — "I've got something to prove" not "I'm the best in the world."
- Don't lean on profanity or chest-thumping. Don't lean on bro-MMA tropes.
- Lean *into* the dad/coach/teacher identity. That's the real differentiator vs. a generic ex-UFC site.

**Words & phrases to keep using**
- "Downtown" (the nickname is the brand)
- "Westside" / Little Rock / Arkansas
- "Break the chain"
- "One at a time" / "Earn it"
- "Lead the way"

**Words & phrases to avoid**
- Anything that frames being released from the UFC as failure. The story is a 12-year career, two UFC bonuses, and a continued fight life across multiple promotions.
- Generic "elite trainer" copy. TJ's gift is that he's a *real fighter who also teaches kids* — that authenticity beats a glossy resume.
